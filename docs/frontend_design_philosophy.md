# Measured Commons

Measured Commons treats the simulation dashboard as an observatory for social pressure, not as a decorative landing page. Space is disciplined and functional: controls are held in a compact side rail, while the main surface is reserved for comparative traces, moral-type contrasts, and group structure. The craft goal is a dense but calm instrument panel that looks meticulously tuned, with every axis, label, and control placed through painstaking attention.

The visual system uses restrained contrast rather than spectacle. Teal, gold, violet, and coral carry distinct moral and metric roles, while the neutral field keeps the interface readable through long repeated runs. The palette avoids one-note symbolism: scarcity, cooperation, fairness, and autonomy should be differentiated by form and position as much as by color. The final work should feel like the product of deep expertise rather than a generic analytics shell.

Charts are not illustrations pasted onto a page; they are the primary visual material. Bars, heat cells, curves, and network nodes should behave like calibrated marks in a social-science field notebook. Text remains sparse and operational: labels, values, scenario IDs, and compact status language. The interface communicates through measured rhythm, repeated visual units, and stable dimensions.

The conceptual thread is the village commons under pressure. Strong organization appears as aligned blocks, free grouping as circular networks, and individual action as variance and drift. Nothing should feel overly theatrical; the drama lives in changing distributions and survival rates. The expected finish is master-level execution: crisp edges, reliable spacing, contained typography, and charts that look labored over with care.
